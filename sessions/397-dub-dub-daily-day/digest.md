## 2. The Schaffer interview — three themes

Jeff interviews **Josh Schaffer** (Senior Director of Platform Technologies; SOTU
host this year). Asked for the SOTU headline, Schaffer gives the year's three
themes:

- **Rebuilt intelligence**
- **Refined platforms**
- **Transformed tools**

### "Transformed tools"

Intelligence is reshaping how development is done, and the tools themselves have
"undergone a huge transformation towards **agentic coding**" — described as making
it much easier to build.

### The "intelligent platform" line

Schaffer unpacks a SOTU line about rich native experiences and intelligent natural-
language interfaces coming together. He splits intelligence into two complementary
layers:

- **In apps** — intelligence makes apps more powerful and adds capabilities "that
  weren't previously even possible."
- **At the system level** — e.g. Siri, making system-wide interactions more
  powerful and making app capabilities accessible in new ways.

His framing: apps stay as important as ever, now amplified by in-app intelligence
and reachable through system-level intelligence.

### What to try first

"Download Xcode and install it, and try out all the new capabilities with agents,
and new ways to code and develop." He calls it "a pretty incredible leap forward."

## 3. The proudest, less-obvious thing — PCC frontier-model access

Asked for something the team is proud of that might not be outwardly obvious,
Schaffer picks **Foundation Models + Private Cloud Compute**. His argument:

> Building with frontier models *seems* like it should be easy, but there's real
> barrier to getting started — you have to figure out a business model to pay for
> token usage, connect to someone's server, and so on.

What he's excited about this year is the ability to **access a frontier model in
Private Cloud Compute with basically no setup or friction at all** — no business
model, no server wiring. He expects it to **democratize access** and unlock apps
that "might not have otherwise even happened" because of the barriers to entry.

> Cross-reference: this is the same PCC story covered in depth in **"What's new in
> the Foundation Models framework"** (session 241 in this workspace) — the
> `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel`, no API keys, free tier under 2M downloads.
